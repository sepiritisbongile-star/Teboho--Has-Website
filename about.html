About the kingdom

A mountain kingdom with a long memory.

Lesotho became independent in 1966, but its story starts earlier, on a flat-topped mountain just outside Maseru.

The land

High ground, thin air

Lesotho is the only country in the world that sits entirely above 1,000 m. Its lowest point is about 1,400 m, and roughly 80% of the land is higher than 1,800 m. That explains the weather: even in summer, evenings are cool, and snow can close the high passes in winter.

Most people live in the lowlands, where Maseru spreads along the Caledon River. The highlands are quieter. Villages are often reached by gravel roads, and in some places a pony is still the most reliable transport.

The country is surrounded entirely by South Africa. It has two official languages, Sesotho and English, and a population of around 2.3 million. Water and diamonds are two of its biggest exports. The Lesotho Highlands Water Project sends water to South Africa and generates electricity at home.

People and culture

Blankets, famo and a printing press

Blankets are not souvenirs here. Basotho blankets are worn daily, especially in the cold months, and the different patterns have names. You will see them at markets in Maseru and Teyateyaneng.

Morija, about 40 km south of Maseru, is one of the oldest mission stations in the country. Its museum and archive hold letters, photographs and newspapers from the 19th century.

Music is part of most celebrations. Famo, with its accordion and drums, is the best-known style. You may hear it from a taxi or a bar long before you see the band.

When to go

Four seasons, one set of mountains

The weather changes quickly at altitude. These are general patterns, not a forecast.

Season Months What to expect
Wet season October to March Green hills, afternoon storms and muddy side roads. Waterfalls are at their best.
Autumn April to May Cooler and clearer, with fewer visitors. Good conditions for hiking.
Winter June to August Snow on high ground and very cold nights. Skiing at Afriski when there is enough snow.
Spring September Windy and dry. Wildflowers start to appear in the lowlands.

What to pack

  • A warm layer, even in summer
  • A waterproof jacket that packs small
  • Cash, because card machines are not everywhere
  • Sunscreen and a hat; the sun is strong at this altitude
  • A 4x4 if you plan to drive Sani Pass
  • A refillable water bottle

Nights in the highlands can drop below freezing between June and August.
